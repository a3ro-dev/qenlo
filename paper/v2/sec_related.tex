\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Power management versus latency.} On CPU servers, low-power states and slow frequency governors are known to inflate the latency of latency-critical services at low load \citep{meisner2011powermgmt,kanev2014tradeoffs}, motivating latency-aware controllers \citep{lo2014pegasus,kasture2015rubik,hsu2015adrenaline,chou2019mudpm}. Mobile governors mispredict concurrent work and run uncoordinated \citep{pathania2014integrated,lin2023geardvfs,zhang2025mobiledvfs}, and at low locked clocks NVIDIA GPUs (V100, Titan, P4) can drop into an idle-like P-state \citep{adamek2021fftedge}. GPU DVFS work for machine learning treats frequency as a knob to \emph{lower} for energy \citep{tang2019gpudvfsdl,you2023zeus,chung2024perseus,qiu2024muserve,stojkovic2025dynamollm,kakolyris2025throttllem,maliakel2025llmdvfs,spaan2026kerneldvfs}. We study the opposite case, in which the default policy under-clocks latency-bound work that the application would prefer to run fast.

The GPU version of this is known to practitioners. NVIDIA's documentation warns that floating clocks make timing-based choices between kernel implementations non-deterministic and that a kernel's measured duration depends on the clock state earlier kernels left behind \citep{nvidia_tensorrt_env,nvidia_ncu}; forum answers describe short, sparse kernels that never raise the clock and recommend locking clocks or keeping the GPU busy with dummy kernels \citep{nvidia_forum_idle}. \citet{volkov2016latency} ran a compute kernel to raise the clock before measuring, in MIG partitions co-located work forces a light partition onto a higher shared clock \citep{patel2023cloudgpu}, and idle gaps between timing trials have corrupted a measured kernel speed-up \citep{agarwal2026kernels}. \citet{chu2026operatorprofiler} note that a lower-power optimized kernel can run at a higher sustained clock and inflate its apparent speed-up, the same class of confound in the power-capped direction. We have not found a controlled study showing that a work-efficient implementation is systematically disadvantaged because the lighter load it places on the GPU induces a lower power state, nor a measurement of a separate low-utilization process speeding up a foreground kernel's device time across hosts.

\paragraph{Benchmark validity.} Innocuous setup details bias measured speed-ups \citep{mytkowicz2009wrongdata}; variation across executions must be sampled and reported \citep{kalibera2013rigorous,maricq2018taming,hoefler2015scientific}. Same-SKU datacenter GPUs vary by 8\% on average (up to 22\%) under sustained compute-heavy load, from power management and manufacturing variability \citep{sinha2022notallgpus}; GEMM timings vary 1.6--14\% across a fleet and 1--6.5\% across repeated runs on one GPU, partly from thermally driven frequency changes \citep{golden2025prism}. Kernel benchmarking tools therefore lock clocks and detect throttling \citep{nvidia_ncu,nvidia_nvbench,oliaro2026fastkernels}, and \texttt{nvidia-smi} power readings cover only part of each update period on some GPUs \citep{yang2023parttime}. Our spread (up to $4.6\times$) is larger than that same-model variability under sustained load, which measures a different regime; it is comparable to informal reports of 2--3$\times$ idle-state slowdowns of short GPU work \citep{nvidia_forum_idle}, and on the pods it confounds host, process and driver.

\paragraph{Filtered vector search and CPU/GPU placement.} Filtered nearest-neighbor search is an active area \citep{gollapudi2023filtered,patel2024acorn,iff2026fanns,shi2025unified,li2025attribute,amanbayev2026systems}, including filtered search on GPUs \citep{xi2025vecflow}; production GPU exact search accepts bitset and bitmap prefilters \citep{nvidia_cuvs_bf}, and GPU $k$-selection has well-studied fast designs \citep{johnson2019billion,zhang2023airtopk} that our single-workgroup selection kernel does not use. Whether to run vector search on a GPU at all depends on data movement and batch size \citep{gregg2011whereisdata,mageirakos2026togpu}; those studies concern transfer-dominated, large-batch regimes, while ours is a resident corpus at batch~1, where per-dispatch overhead is tens of microseconds on Vulkan \citep{maczan2026webgpu}. Compile-time CPU/GPU operator placement is fragile when estimates or co-processor resources are wrong \citep{bress2016robust,karnagel2017adaptive,rosenfeld2022survey}. The choice between an eligible-id list and a full-width filter resembles selection vectors versus bitmaps in vectorized CPU databases \citep{ngom2021filter}, although our two GPU paths also differ in their selection work. We add that the measured margins between such alternatives can depend on the GPU's power state, which the alternatives themselves influence.
